Why solar warranties matter

Solar panels are built to last, but manufacturing defects, premature degradation, or inverter failures can occur. A solid warranty protects you financially and gives you peace of mind. Unlike a standard appliance, a solar system consists of multiple components—panels, inverter, mounting hardware, and sometimes a battery—each with its own warranty terms. Understanding these layers is the first step to safeguarding your investment.

Types of solar warranties

1. Product warranty (equipment)

This covers defects in materials and workmanship. For solar panels, product warranties typically range from 10 to 15 years (sometimes up to 25 years for premium brands). Inverters usually come with 5–12 years, depending on the manufacturer. Always check whether the warranty is transferable if you sell your home—this adds resale value.

2. Performance warranty (power output)

Solar panels degrade slowly over time—that’s normal. A performance warranty guarantees that your panels will produce at least a certain percentage of their original rated power after 25 years (usually 80–85%). This is a long‑term assurance that your system will keep generating savings. Some tier‑1 manufacturers offer linear performance warranties that are more predictable than stepped ones.

3. Labour and installation warranty

Even the best equipment can fail if installed incorrectly. A workmanship warranty (often called an installation warranty) covers the labour and installation quality. In Australia, the Clean Energy Council recommends a minimum of 5 years for installation work. This is distinct from the product warranty, and you should confirm both are clearly stated in your contract.

What a good solar warranty should include

  • Clear terms and conditions: Avoid warranties with vague language or excessive exclusions.
  • Transferability: If you sell your property, the warranty should ideally transfer to the new owner.
  • Australian presence: A local support team makes claims faster and easier to resolve.
  • Coverage for labour and transport: Some warranties cover only the replacement part, not the cost of getting it installed.
  • Reputable manufacturers: Tier‑1 panel brands and established inverter companies are more likely to honour long‑term warranties.

Common pitfalls to avoid

Many homeowners assume all warranties are created equal. However, some companies go out of business, leaving warranties worthless. Others bury important conditions in fine print—such as requiring annual maintenance or using specific cleaning products. Always read the warranty document before signing, and ask these questions:

  • What is the exact duration of each warranty (product, performance, labour)?
  • Who is responsible for honouring the warranty—the installer, the manufacturer, or both?
  • Are there any maintenance requirements that could void the warranty?
  • What is the claims process, and how long does it typically take?

How to make a warranty claim

If you notice a drop in performance, physical damage, or an inverter error, start by contacting your installer or manufacturer. Provide your system’s serial numbers, installation date, and a description of the issue. Most reputable companies will guide you through the process, arrange an inspection, and replace faulty components if covered. Keep all documentation—invoices, warranty certificates, and maintenance records—in a safe place.

Final thoughts

Solar is a long‑term investment, and a strong warranty is your best protection. Don’t just compare panel prices—compare warranty terms. A slightly higher upfront cost for premium equipment with a 25‑year product warranty and a 30‑year performance guarantee can save you thousands down the line.
